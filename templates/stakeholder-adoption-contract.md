## Fictional worked example

Meridian Ledger believes developers need a consolidated evidence dashboard.
Interviews appear favorable, especially among early enthusiasts. Observation
across four work classes reveals different constraints:

- one cohort cannot interpret scattered evidence and benefits from the draft;
- one lacks stable test data, so a dashboard reorganizes absence;
- one understands evidence but cannot identify the consequence owner;
- one avoids the factory because its exception process is unclear.

The platform team pilots the dashboard only with the first cohort, creates
separate discovery for test data and ownership, and repairs the exception path.
The decision succeeds by preventing one assumed need from becoming a mandatory
enterprise product.

## Research boundary

This artifact applies public GOV.UK guidance on continuous user research,
whole-team participation, end-to-end journeys, and turning assumptions into
research questions. Applying those service-design principles to an internal
software factory is a design inference to test in context.
