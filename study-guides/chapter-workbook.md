# Chapter-by-Chapter Applied Workbook

Read the chapter before completing its exercise. The prompts below test
application and judgment; they do not summarize or replace the book's argument,
Meridian Ledger consequences, or architectural tradeoffs.

For every chapter keep one learning record:

- chapter and date;
- claim you can now explain;
- local example or counterexample;
- artifact created or changed;
- unhappy path tested;
- evidence observed;
- decision that still requires a person; and
- question or contradiction to revisit.

## Chapter 1 — Code Is No Longer the Scarce Output

**Apply:** List ten requested software changes. Classify the actual constraint
after generation: demand, clarity, evidence, review, integration, operation,
outcome learning, or retirement.

**Prove:** Identify one item where faster code would increase inventory rather
than justified throughput.

**Discuss:** What becomes more valuable when candidate production becomes cheap?

## Chapter 2 — From Tool Adoption to Factory Operation

**Apply:** Trace one AI-assisted change from request through continuing
obligation. Mark every informal handoff and missing owner.

**Prove:** Show the earliest point where the system can no longer reconstruct
why the change was allowed.

**Discuss:** Which responsibilities belong to a factory even when every model
and agent component is purchased?

## Chapter 3 — The Factory Contract

**Apply:** Complete the
[`factory charter`](../templates/factory-charter-template.md) for one narrow
work class.

**Prove:** Present one plausible ineligible request and the exact refusal path.

**Discuss:** Who may change the contract, and why cannot execution do so?

## Chapter 4 — Intent Is an Engineering Artifact

**Apply:** Rewrite one ambiguous ticket as a Work Order with claims,
prohibitions, assumptions, countermetrics, and unresolved decisions.

**Prove:** Ask another person to identify what must be true without explaining
the ticket verbally.

**Discuss:** Which implementation details are suggestions rather than intent?

## Chapter 5 — Work Orders and the Decision Chain

**Apply:** Map intent → attempt → candidate → evidence → disposition → effect →
receipt → outcome for one completed change.

**Prove:** Reconstruct the decision without a transcript or the original author.

**Discuss:** Where does provenance become organizational memory rather than log
volume?

## Chapter 6 — Memory Must Earn Its Influence

**Apply:** Complete the
[`memory governance workbook`](../templates/memory-governance-workbook.md) for
one retrospective lesson.

**Prove:** Retrieve the lesson outside its scope and show why it cannot affect
the new work.

**Discuss:** What evidence would contradict or retire it?

## Chapter 7 — Bounded Execution

**Apply:** List data, tools, writes, environment, communication, budget, time,
concurrency, delegation, and duration separately.

**Prove:** Run the persuasive authority-escape drill.

**Discuss:** Which boundaries are only prompt instructions today?

## Chapter 8 — One Agent, Many Agents, and the Cost of Handoffs

**Apply:** Compare one owner, staged specialists, owner-led fan-out, and peer
coordination for the same work.

**Prove:** Account for integration, shared budget, cancellation, lineage, and
review capacity—not model latency alone.

**Discuss:** What evidence would justify adding the second agent?

## Chapter 9 — Deterministic Machinery Around Probabilistic Work

**Apply:** Run the reference factory. For each rejection, name the promise the
deterministic rule protects.

**Prove:** Attempt one illegal state transition directly.

**Discuss:** When can a deterministic rule be confidently wrong?

## Chapter 10 — The Evidence Plane

**Apply:** Complete the
[`risk-to-evidence matrix`](../templates/risk-to-evidence-matrix.md) for three
material claims made by one candidate.

**Prove:** Change the candidate and invalidate stale verdicts.

**Discuss:** How much evaluator independence does each claim warrant?

## Chapter 11 — The Control Plane

**Apply:** Complete the
[`decision-rights matrix`](../templates/decision-rights-matrix.md) for grant,
exception, release, rollback, outcome interpretation, and retirement.

**Prove:** Show that production cannot mint one of those rights.

**Discuss:** Which emergency authority expires automatically?

## Chapter 12 — Observability, Provenance, and Recovery

**Apply:** Draw the reconciliation path for one effectful adapter.

**Prove:** Run the lost-response drill without creating a duplicate effect.

**Discuss:** Which signals tell an operator what to do rather than merely what
happened?

## Chapter 13 — Humans Are the Judgment Architecture

**Apply:** Complete the
[`human judgment worksheet`](../templates/human-judgment-placement.md).

**Prove:** Remove the designated reviewer and observe declared absence behavior.

**Discuss:** What competence, attention, authority, and consequence does each
human decision require?

## Chapter 14 — The Factory as an Internal Product

**Apply:** Describe the service promise, supported exit, stop message,
escalation, degraded modes, and support owner for one work class.

**Prove:** Give a stopped user enough evidence and direction to proceed safely.

**Discuss:** Where has the paved road become a tollbooth or hidden queue?

## Chapter 15 — Measure Outcomes, Not Machine Activity

**Apply:** Pre-register a primary outcome, countermetrics, baseline,
denominators, window, and decision rule.

**Prove:** Produce a technically successful but outcome-insufficient result.

**Discuss:** Which favorable metric transfers burden to another person or time?

## Chapter 16 — The Factory That Improves the Factory

**Apply:** Complete the
[`continuous improvement experiment`](../templates/continuous-improvement-experiment.md)
for one recurring failure.

**Prove:** Reject or narrow a self-improvement proposal without losing its
evidence.

**Discuss:** Can the proposal alter the test that judges it?

## Chapter 17 — The New Economics of Ownership

**Apply:** Fix the outcome and non-negotiable requirements, reject options that
fail the common bar, then compare lifecycle ownership among the survivors.

**Prove:** Price one compensating seam, remove one provider, and show which
records, authority, and recovery paths remain portable.

**Discuss:** Which internal workaround proves that a purchase relocated rather
than eliminated ownership?

## Chapter 18 — The First Ninety Days

**Apply:** Complete the
[`ninety-day pilot workbook`](../templates/ninety-day-pilot-workbook.md) and
diagnostic for one work class.

**Prove:** Write a day-ninety memo capable of expanding, holding, narrowing,
redesigning, or stopping authority.

**Discuss:** Which single dimension may change next, and what would contract it?

## Cohort capstone

Use the [`Meridian Ledger complete trace`](../examples/meridian-ledger-complete-trace.md)
as a red-team exercise. Assign intent owner, producer, evaluator, security,
release, operations, consequence owner, and finance roles. Give each role
different evidence. Require the group to preserve dissent and issue one
decision another cohort can reconstruct.
