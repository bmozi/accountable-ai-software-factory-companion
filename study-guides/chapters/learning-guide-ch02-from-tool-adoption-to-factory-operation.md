# Learning Guide — Chapter 2: From Tool Adoption to Factory Operation

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Distinguish individual assistance, workflows, platforms, and factory operation.
> 2. Evaluate factory maturity using evidence rather than adoption activity.
> 3. Analyze readiness across intent, state, evidence, control, recovery, and ownership.
> 4. Identify the weakest responsibility in a proposed factory operating model.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Individual assistance:** AI use governed mainly by a person's local practice.
- **Workflow:** Coordinated steps for completing a bounded task.
- **Platform:** Shared services and controls used across teams.
- **Factory operation:** An operating system for governed change and learning.
- **Readiness:** Evidence that foundational responsibilities can support expanded use.

### Review Questions

1. How does a workflow differ from a factory?
2. Why does widespread adoption fail to establish maturity?
3. Which responsibilities make factory operation sociotechnical?
4. Why should the weakest responsibility guide the next investment?

### Discussion Questions

1. Can an organization have high tool adoption and low factory readiness?
2. When does platform standardization become harmful uniformity?

### Exercises

**Exercise 2.1 (Core): Classify the current state.** Place three existing AI practices in the assistance, workflow, platform, or factory state.
*Deliverable:* A classification table with evidence.
*Assessment:* Each placement cites observable operating responsibilities rather than branding.

**Exercise 2.2 (Core): Find the weak responsibility.** Evaluate one flow across intent, state, evidence, control, recovery, outcomes, learning, and ownership.
*Deliverable:* An evidence-based maturity assessment.
*Assessment:* The weakest consequential responsibility is explicit and supported.

**Exercise 2.3 (Challenge): Design the next capability.** Propose the smallest change that strengthens the weak responsibility without prematurely expanding authority.
*Deliverable:* A bounded capability proposal.
*Assessment:* The proposal defines proof, owner, failure path, and reversal condition.
