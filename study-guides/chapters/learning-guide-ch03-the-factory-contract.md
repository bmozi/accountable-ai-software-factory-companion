# Learning Guide — Chapter 3: The Factory Contract

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Define the responsibilities governed by a factory contract.
> 2. Separate authority dimensions that are often collapsed into one permission.
> 3. Evaluate whether evidence, stopping, ownership, and learning rules are executable.
> 4. Construct and test a bounded factory charter.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Factory contract:** The executable agreement governing a factory capability.
- **Eligibility:** The conditions under which work may enter the factory.
- **Authority:** The actions and consequences a system is permitted to create.
- **Evidence floor:** The minimum proof required before a state transition.
- **Stopping rule:** A condition that prohibits continued automated action.

### Review Questions

1. Which responsibilities belong in a factory contract?
2. Why must authority be separated into dimensions?
3. What makes a stopping rule operational rather than aspirational?
4. Why must the contract govern learning as well as execution?

### Discussion Questions

1. How much contract detail is enough before a first pilot?
2. Who should be allowed to change the factory contract, and under what evidence?

### Exercises

**Exercise 3.1 (Core): Draft a charter.** Use the companion template for one reversible work class.
*Deliverable:* A completed factory charter.
*Assessment:* All eligibility, authority, evidence, stop, recovery, learning, and ownership fields are decidable.

**Exercise 3.2 (Core): Run a reader test.** Ask two authorized readers to decide the same six boundary questions independently.
*Deliverable:* A disagreement log and revision.
*Assessment:* Material disagreements are resolved in the artifact rather than conversation alone.

**Exercise 3.3 (Challenge): Test refusal.** Design a safe attempt to enter ineligible work or cross a prohibited authority boundary.
*Deliverable:* A refusal-test plan and expected receipt.
*Assessment:* Enforcement, operator visibility, escalation, and preserved state are specified.
