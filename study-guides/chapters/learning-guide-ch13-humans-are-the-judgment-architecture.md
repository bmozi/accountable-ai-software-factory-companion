# Learning Guide — Chapter 13: Humans Are the Judgment Architecture

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Explain why human involvement should be placed by decision need, not habit.
> 2. Analyze decisions by stakes, ambiguity, irreversibility, and value conflict.
> 3. Evaluate whether a judgment interface supplies competence, evidence, time, and authority.
> 4. Construct a deliberate human-judgment map for one work class.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Judgment:** An accountable choice where facts and rules do not force one answer.
- **Human attention:** Scarce cognitive capacity that must be allocated by risk.
- **Judgment interface:** The evidence, options, authority, and time presented to a decision-maker.
- **Value conflict:** A decision involving legitimate but competing priorities.
- **Independence:** Freedom to challenge a path despite aligned incentives or momentum.

### Review Questions

1. Why is a human in every loop not a strategy?
2. Which four conditions most strongly call for human judgment?
3. What makes a judgment interface effective?
4. Why does management remain after automation expands?

### Discussion Questions

1. Which current approvals are ceremony, and which require courage?
2. How should an organization respond when no qualified human can review within the required time?

### Exercises

**Exercise 13.1 (Core): Place judgment.** Rate each decision in one flow by stakes, ambiguity, irreversibility, and value conflict.
*Deliverable:* A human-judgment map.
*Assessment:* Human and mechanical roles follow decision need rather than tradition.

**Exercise 13.2 (Core): Redesign an approval.** Improve the evidence, alternatives, authority, and response expectation for one reviewer.
*Deliverable:* A judgment-interface specification.
*Assessment:* The reviewer can stop, redirect, or approve with owned consequence.

**Exercise 13.3 (Challenge): Test independence.** Create a scenario where schedule and leadership incentives favor an unsafe choice.
*Deliverable:* An escalation and dissent protocol.
*Assessment:* A qualified challenger can delay consequence without relying on personal heroics.
