# Learning Guide — Chapter 16: The Factory That Improves the Factory

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Explain why learning may earn influence but cannot inherit authority.
> 2. Analyze a finding through admission, experiment, evaluation, and disposition.
> 3. Evaluate improvement proposals for hidden authority expansion and metric gaming.
> 4. Construct a controlled experiment for one recurring factory failure.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Finding:** An observed condition that may justify investigation.
- **Experiment admission:** The decision to permit a bounded improvement test.
- **Treatment:** The controlled change evaluated by an experiment.
- **Promotion:** Authorization for a validated change to influence normal operation.
- **Approval floor:** A decision boundary the improvement loop cannot lower itself.

### Review Questions

1. Why does learning not automatically receive authority?
2. Which dispositions should be recorded separately?
3. How can an improvement change authority indirectly?
4. Why should continuous improvement include deletion?

### Discussion Questions

1. Who should be independent from a factory's self-improvement loop?
2. When should a useful local optimization be rejected because system complexity rises?

### Exercises

**Exercise 16.1 (Core): Govern one experiment.** Use the companion template for a recurring failure.
*Deliverable:* A completed experiment record.
*Assessment:* Admission, treatment, outcome, countermetric, and disposition are separate.

**Exercise 16.2 (Core): Audit authority drift.** Identify every way the treatment could widen future capability.
*Deliverable:* An authority-impact review.
*Assessment:* Indirect expansion requires an explicit owner and approval floor.

**Exercise 16.3 (Challenge): Design for deletion.** Select one rule, prompt, check, or handoff that might be retired.
*Deliverable:* A retirement experiment.
*Assessment:* Coverage, countermetric, rollback, observation window, and audit preservation are defined.
