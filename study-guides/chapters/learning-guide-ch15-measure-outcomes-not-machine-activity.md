# Learning Guide — Chapter 15: Measure Outcomes, Not Machine Activity

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Distinguish activity, delivery events, and observable outcomes.
> 2. Pair primary measures with countermetrics and bounded baselines.
> 3. Analyze tails, stopping quality, and displaced burden in a scorecard.
> 4. Construct an outcome contract that leads to an explicit disposition.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Activity metric:** A count of work performed without proof of effect.
- **Outcome measure:** An observation of a changed customer, system, or organizational condition.
- **Countermetric:** A measure of likely harm or displaced burden.
- **Baseline:** A dated comparison condition with stated scope and limitations.
- **Disposition:** The accountable decision made after reviewing evidence.

### Review Questions

1. Why are tokens and pull requests weak outcome measures?
2. What purpose does a countermetric serve?
3. Why should a scorecard inspect the tail?
4. Which dispositions can follow an observation window?

### Discussion Questions

1. Which attractive productivity metric would you refuse to use alone?
2. Who should own the decision when primary outcomes improve but countermetrics worsen?

### Exercises

**Exercise 15.1 (Core): Pair outcome and harm.** Select no more than three primary measures and a countermetric for each.
*Deliverable:* An outcome-and-countermetric table.
*Assessment:* Measures are observable, bounded, and tied to a decision.

**Exercise 15.2 (Core): Inspect the tail.** Compare the five worst baseline cases with the proposed scorecard.
*Deliverable:* A tail-visibility audit.
*Assessment:* The scorecard cannot hide severe delay, rework, or harm behind an average.

**Exercise 15.3 (Challenge): Write disposition memos.** Draft preserve, expand, narrow, and stop decisions for the same capability.
*Deliverable:* Four one-paragraph decision memos.
*Assessment:* Each memo follows evidence, limitations, countermetrics, and named ownership.
