# Learning Guide — Chapter 17: The New Economics of Ownership

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Separate commodity capability from differentiating organizational judgment.
> 2. Apply a fixed requirements gate before comparing ownership or cost.
> 3. Expose workarounds and glue code as owned seams rather than free fit.
> 4. Evaluate components by authority, evidence, degraded mode, and exit cost.
> 5. Analyze build, buy, assemble, replace-a-slice, and buy-to-learn choices as a capability portfolio.
> 6. Construct a Fit-Before-Ownership Ledger that preserves internal accountability.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Capability map:** A portfolio view of the functions the factory requires.
- **Requirements gate:** A pass, fail, or unknown test against a fixed set of non-negotiable outcomes and constraints.
- **Seam ledger:** A record of the workarounds, owners, coupled systems, break triggers, recovery, and recurring load created by fit gaps.
- **Differentiating layer:** Organizational judgment or capability worth owning directly.
- **Stable contract:** An interface that preserves accountability across replaceable components.
- **Degraded mode:** Safe operation when a component is unavailable or untrusted.
- **Exit cost:** The effort and consequence of replacing or removing a component.

### Review Questions

1. Why must fit be evaluated before total cost of ownership?
2. How can a purchase relocate rather than eliminate internal building?
3. Why is a fast AI-built prototype evidence about feasibility but not proof of operability?
4. Which capabilities are strongest candidates for internal ownership?
5. What evidence should remain inside the organization?
6. Why must exit be tested before it is needed?

### Discussion Questions

1. When can vendor maturity outweigh loss of direct control?
2. When is buy-to-learn a real option strategy rather than deferred lock-in?
3. Which attractive integration convenience creates the greatest future lock-in?

### Exercises

**Exercise 17.1 (Core): Apply the requirements gate.** Define the fixed outcome, non-negotiable requirements, evidence floor, authority boundary, degraded mode, and stopping conditions for one sourcing decision. Mark each complete option pass, fail, or unknown.
*Deliverable:* A requirements-gate table.
*Assessment:* Cost and ownership preference do not rescue an option that fails a non-negotiable requirement.

**Exercise 17.2 (Core): Price the seam.** Choose one fit gap in a purchased or assembled option and record its compensation, owner, coupled systems, break trigger, detection, recovery, recurring load, and removal path.
*Deliverable:* A seam-ledger row and lifecycle-cost update.
*Assessment:* The workaround is counted as part of the option rather than hidden as free internal work.

**Exercise 17.3 (Challenge): Compare the survivors and rehearse reversal.** Build a capability portfolio for the options that passed, then define how to replace or retire the selected component while preserving decisions and provenance.
*Deliverable:* A Fit-Before-Ownership Ledger with a tested-exit plan.
*Assessment:* Internal obligation, seam cost, data portability, contract compatibility, authority removal, and recovery are explicit and verifiable.
