# Learning Guide — Chapter 17: Build, Buy, or Assemble

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Separate commodity capability from differentiating organizational judgment.
> 2. Evaluate components by authority, evidence, degraded mode, and exit cost.
> 3. Analyze build, buy, and assemble choices as a capability portfolio.
> 4. Construct a capability map that preserves internal accountability.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Capability map:** A portfolio view of the functions the factory requires.
- **Differentiating layer:** Organizational judgment or capability worth owning directly.
- **Stable contract:** An interface that preserves accountability across replaceable components.
- **Degraded mode:** Safe operation when a component is unavailable or untrusted.
- **Exit cost:** The effort and consequence of replacing or removing a component.

### Review Questions

1. Why is build versus buy the wrong level for one global decision?
2. Which capabilities are strongest candidates for internal ownership?
3. What evidence should remain inside the organization?
4. Why must exit be tested before it is needed?

### Discussion Questions

1. When can vendor maturity outweigh loss of direct control?
2. Which attractive integration convenience creates the greatest future lock-in?

### Exercises

**Exercise 17.1 (Core): Build a capability map.** Rate factory capabilities by differentiation, sensitivity, competence, maturity, integration, and exit.
*Deliverable:* A build-buy-assemble portfolio.
*Assessment:* Each decision is capability-specific and names internal ownership.

**Exercise 17.2 (Core): Price failure.** Model one vendor outage and one confident incorrect result.
*Deliverable:* A degraded-mode and detection plan.
*Assessment:* Independent evidence and continuity do not depend on the failed component.

**Exercise 17.3 (Challenge): Rehearse exit.** Define how to replace a selected component while preserving decisions and provenance.
*Deliverable:* A tested-exit plan.
*Assessment:* Data portability, contract compatibility, authority removal, and recovery are verifiable.
