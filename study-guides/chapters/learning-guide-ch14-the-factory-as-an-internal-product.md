# Learning Guide — Chapter 14: The Factory as an Internal Product

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Define the factory as an internal product with users and service obligations.
> 2. Analyze paved roads, exits, support boundaries, and failure recovery.
> 3. Evaluate adoption as a signal without treating it as proof of value.
> 4. Construct a two-path service blueprint for success and controlled stopping.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Internal product:** A managed capability serving users inside the organization.
- **Paved road:** A supported default path that makes safe work easier.
- **Exit:** A legitimate route for work the default path should not force.
- **Service boundary:** A clear division of responsibility and support ownership.
- **Developer experience:** The usability conditions that influence reliable adoption.

### Review Questions

1. Why is adoption not proof of product value?
2. What makes a paved road trustworthy?
3. How do service boundaries protect the platform team?
4. Why should stopped work have a designed experience?

### Discussion Questions

1. When should a platform preserve friction rather than remove it?
2. How can teams retain autonomy without fragmenting the factory contract?

### Exercises

**Exercise 14.1 (Core): Blueprint success and stop.** Map user action, interface, backstage service, evidence, policy, support, and recovery.
*Deliverable:* A two-path service blueprint.
*Assessment:* Every handoff names owner, evidence, response expectation, and recovery.

**Exercise 14.2 (Core): Diagnose support burden.** Classify recurring requests as eliminate, explain, automate, or preserve.
*Deliverable:* A support-demand disposition table.
*Assessment:* Each choice considers user value and displaced burden.

**Exercise 14.3 (Challenge): Design earned adoption.** Propose staged availability for one capability.
*Deliverable:* An adoption-and-authority plan.
*Assessment:* Expansion depends on outcomes, countermetrics, and a revocation path.
