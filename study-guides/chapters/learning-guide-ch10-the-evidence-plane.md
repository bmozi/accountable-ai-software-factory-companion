# Learning Guide — Chapter 10: The Evidence Plane

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Explain why evidence must remain distinct from generation and approval.
> 2. Match evidence strength and independence to a claim's consequence.
> 3. Analyze disagreement, staleness, and operational feedback as evidence conditions.
> 4. Construct a risk-to-evidence matrix with an explicit disagreement rule.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Evidence plane:** The mechanisms that test and preserve claims about a candidate.
- **Evidence independence:** Separation between production and evaluation reasoning paths.
- **Evidence floor:** Minimum acceptable proof for a decision.
- **Disagreement rule:** The predetermined action when evidence conflicts.
- **Operational evidence:** Post-release observation of outcomes and harms.

### Review Questions

1. Why is a verdict word not an evidence package?
2. How should evidence strength relate to consequence?
3. What causes evidence to become stale?
4. Why does the evidence plane continue after release?

### Discussion Questions

1. How much independence is enough for low-risk versus high-risk claims?
2. What should happen when deterministic tests and expert judgment disagree?

### Exercises

**Exercise 10.1 (Core): Map risk to evidence.** Use the companion matrix for one proposed change.
*Deliverable:* A claim-level evidence plan.
*Assessment:* Consequence and uncertainty justify each evidence source.

**Exercise 10.2 (Core): Precommit disagreement.** Define actions for conflicting evidence before checks run.
*Deliverable:* A disagreement protocol.
*Assessment:* The unfavorable result cannot be waived without a new accountable decision.

**Exercise 10.3 (Challenge): Extend evidence after release.** Connect one pre-release claim to canary, telemetry, rollback, and learning signals.
*Deliverable:* An evidence lifecycle map.
*Assessment:* Staleness, observation window, owner, and correction path are explicit.
