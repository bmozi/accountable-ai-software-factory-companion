# Learning Guide — Chapter 6: Memory Must Earn Its Influence

> **Learning Objectives** — After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
>
> 1. Distinguish source records, observations, candidates, validated learning, and guidance.
> 2. Explain how provenance, contradiction, decay, and retirement govern memory.
> 3. Evaluate memory by the quality of decisions it influences.
> 4. Construct a governed learning lifecycle for one operational lesson.

## Study Guide

### Key Terms

- **Source record:** An artifact or event preserved as evidence of what occurred.
- **Candidate learning:** A proposed lesson not yet authorized to influence normal work.
- **Provenance:** The trace of origin, transformations, evidence, and approval.
- **Contradiction:** Material evidence that cannot safely be merged into one claim.
- **Retirement:** Removal of guidance from normal influence while preserving audit history.

### Review Questions

1. Which distinct states are commonly collapsed into memory?
2. Why is contradiction useful rather than merely an error?
3. How can forgetting improve reliability?
4. Why is a memory write an authority decision?

### Discussion Questions

1. Should useful guidance require a scheduled expiration by default?
2. What evidence could show that retrieval quality improved while decisions worsened?

### Exercises

**Exercise 6.1 (Core): Govern one learning.** Use the companion workbook to classify a retrospective lesson.
*Deliverable:* A learning lifecycle record.
*Assessment:* Source, scope, evidence, approver, contradiction, decay, and retirement are explicit.

**Exercise 6.2 (Core): Construct a contradiction test.** Create two plausible sources that disagree across time or scope.
*Deliverable:* A retrieval and escalation test.
*Assessment:* The system preserves both sources and avoids unauthorized synthesis.

**Exercise 6.3 (Challenge): Measure influence.** Define how to test whether one memory improves the decision it informs.
*Deliverable:* An evaluation design with a countermetric.
*Assessment:* Success depends on decision quality, not sentence retrieval alone.
